A spec sheet that gets you a real quote in one reply

Most first enquiries arrive as a photo and one line: "how much for these?" That starts a conversation of ten messages before anyone can quote, and every round trip costs a day.

The nine things a factory actually needs are: fibre, gauge (or a sock to match), height, size range, colour count with Pantone references, logo method and placement, artwork file, packing, and the first order quantity with a target ship date. None of them are difficult to answer, and all of them change the price.

We put them on a two-page sheet. Fill it in, send it with the logo file, and the reply is a price band, three yarn options and a mock-up date - not another question. The sheet also doubles as an internal document: if two people in your team fill it in differently, that disagreement is worth finding before the sample, not after.

Height is the item most often left blank, and it is the one that changes everything downstream: yarn count, machine time, band and price. "Sock" is not a height. Six heights cover everything we knit, from a no-show to a thigh-high.

The same enquiry, written two ways

Version one, which is what most first emails look like: a photo of a sock and the line "how much for these, 500 pieces". A factory receiving that can answer none of the questions that set the price. Which height is it? How many colours in the artwork? Is the logo knitted, embroidered or printed? What packing? The reply has to be a question, and every question costs a day.

Version two: fibre, gauge or a sock to match, height with a measurement, size range, colour count with Pantone references, logo method and placement, artwork file, packing, first quantity and target ship date. Nine lines. The reply is a price band, three yarn options and a mock-up date, because there is nothing left to ask.

The difference is not politeness or paperwork. A sock price is mostly machine time, and machine time is decided by the height, the colour count and the logo method. Without those three the honest answer is a range so wide it is useless; with them the range narrows to a number that can go into a quotation with the trade term beside it.

There is a second use for the sheet that buyers discover later. If two people on your team fill it in and produce different heights or different colour counts, that disagreement has surfaced before sampling instead of after bulk. Finding it on paper costs nothing; finding it on a pallet costs a season.

The sheet lives on the customization page as a two-page download. Filling it in takes about five minutes and saves roughly a week of email — which is usually the difference between sampling this month and sampling next.
