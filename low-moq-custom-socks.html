Why a floor exists

Setup is the fixed cost, not the yarn.

Before a single sock exists, a design has to be programmed, a cylinder selected and mounted, the feeders threaded and the needle selection set for the pattern. That work is identical whether the machine then runs 100 pairs or 10,000. At six designs you pay it six times — which is why the minimum counts per programme rather than per order.

Yarn carries its own minimums on top. A colour that is not in stock has to be dyed, and a dyehouse runs a batch rather than a pair. Below a certain quantity per colour you are either choosing the nearest stock shade or paying for dye you will not use.

Capacity

Our own floor, and our partner capacity.

Two kinds of capacity behind one specification.

We run twenty to thirty circular knitting machines on one floor in Yuanhua Town, Jiaxing. Knitting, linking, grip application, finishing and packing all happen in that building, which is why a short run moves quickly here: a change request is a walk across the room.

For larger volumes we work with partner factories we have kept for years. That is what lets a seasonal programme land without stretching the calendar, and it is why a big order and a test order can sit side by side without one delaying the other.

What does not change is the specification. The same yarn, the same construction and a pre-production sample you approve before bulk starts — so the pair that reaches you is the pair you signed off, whichever set of needles made it.

Questions buyers ask

Before you send artwork

Straight answers, no sales copy.

Is 100 pairs really the minimum?

Yes — 100 pairs per style per colour, on every height. That holds across all four constructions: jacquard, embroidery, sublimation print and grip. A knee-high or a thigh-high costs more per pair than an ankle sock — more yarn — but the minimum does not move.

Do all four constructions run at 100 pairs?

Yes. A jacquard design with six colours, an embroidered logo, a full-bleed sublimation print and a silicone or PVC grip sole all have the same floor. What changes between them is the setup work, not the minimum.

What if I want six designs?

The minimum counts per style and per colourway, so six designs is six programmes rather than one order of 600. Ask instead whether a setup can carry across colourways of the same structure — often it can, and that is a genuinely different number.

Can I split sizes inside one 100-pair order?

Yes. The 100 pairs can be spread across sizes inside one style and one colour. Send the split before production starts, because it is written into the order confirmation.

What happens on a much larger order?

Yes. Our own floor is built for short runs; for bigger volumes we work with partner factories we have kept for years, which is what keeps the calendar flexible when a season arrives at once. The yarn, the specification and the approved pre-production sample are the same either way.

Will you make two pairs for an individual?

No, and the honest reason is arithmetic: the programming and machine setting are the same whether the machine runs two pairs or 200. Below 100 pairs a print-on-demand service that prints onto a blank sock is the better tool, and we will say so rather than take an order we cannot run well.

Send the artwork or a photo of a sock you like.

You get a price band, three yarn options and a mock-up date back within 24 hours. If 100 pairs is the wrong size of order for what you are doing, we will tell you that too.
