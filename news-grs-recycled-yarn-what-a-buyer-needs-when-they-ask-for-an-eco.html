GRS recycled yarn: what a buyer needs when they ask for an eco certificate

More first enquiries now say "eco certificate", and the request usually hides a narrower question: can I print a recycled claim, and can I back it up when a retailer asks. Those are two different documents.

GRS, the Global Recycled Standard run by Textile Exchange, is the one most programmes mean. It is verified by independent certification bodies rather than self-declared, and it sets a floor: 20 percent recycled content to be certified at all, 50 percent before the product itself may carry the GRS logo.

Two documents do the work, and they get confused constantly. The scope certificate belongs to a company, not to an order: it lists the products and processes that company is certified for and expires annually. It proves the spinner is inside the system. It proves nothing about your shipment. The transaction certificate belongs to one shipment — seller, buyer, product, quantity — and is issued once the goods move. That is the document that proves your order contains what you paid for.

So the chain matters more than any single PDF. Feedstock, spinner, dyer, knitter: each link needs its own scope certificate, and a gap anywhere means no transaction certificate can be issued at the end. If a supplier sends one certificate for the whole chain, ask which link it covers. Then check three things before the claim goes on a box: the scope certificate is current and its product list covers the yarn you are buying, the transaction certificate matches this order rather than a previous one, and the percentage printed on the pack is the percentage the certificate states.

One honest note about where we sit. Like OEKO-TEX, GRS is normally held upstream — by the yarn mill, not by the knitter. We knit; the recycled content lives in the yarn we buy. Raise it at enquiry stage and we will tell you plainly whether the yarn we can source for your design carries it. We will not put a GRS mark on a sock unless the transaction certificate covers that order, because a mark is worth nothing the first time a retailer asks for the backup.

RCS, the Recycled Claim Standard, is the lighter option: it traces recycled content at a much lower threshold and without the GRS social and environmental criteria. If all you need is a verified percentage it is cheaper and faster. If your buyer is a European retailer with a published standard, it is usually GRS.
