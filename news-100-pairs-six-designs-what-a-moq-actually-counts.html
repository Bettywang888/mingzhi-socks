100 pairs, six designs: what a MOQ actually counts

The question that arrives most often from new brands is some version of this: I want six designs, can I get the 100-pair minimum across the whole order. The answer is no, and the reason is not greed — it is that the minimum is counting something specific.

A minimum order quantity counts per style, per colourway. Six designs at 100 pairs each is an order for 600 pairs across six programmes, not one programme of 600. None of that is negotiable in a knitting mill, for three concrete reasons.

Setting up is the fixed cost. Programming the design, selecting and mounting the cylinder, threading the feeders, setting needle selection for the pattern: the machine is not making socks during any of it. That work is identical whether you then run 100 pairs or 10,000. At six designs you pay it six times.

Yarn has its own minimums. A colour that is not in stock has to be dyed, and a dyehouse runs a batch, not a pair. Below a certain quantity per colour you are either buying a stock shade close to your target, or paying for dye you will not use.

Dye lots exist. Two runs of the same colour produced weeks apart will not match exactly. If your six designs share a base colour, producing them together is cheaper and more consistent than producing them separately — which is a reason to place one order, not six.

So the practical pattern: decide how many styles you can genuinely sell, then pick the two or three that matter and put the quantity there. A brand that launches with six designs at 100 pairs each usually finds that four of them do not sell, and the setup money spent on those is gone.

There is a better question than "can you lower the minimum": can the setup carry across colourways of the same structure? Same gauge, same yarn count, same toe, different colours only is often a genuinely different number from six unrelated designs. That question gets a useful answer. The other one gets a no.

We quote per style per colour from 100 pairs, and the customization page sets out what counts as a change of style. If you are planning a first collection, send the list and we will tell you which of them actually need separate setups.

Height does not move that floor. A knee-high or a thigh-high starts at 100 pairs per colour exactly like a crew sock — what changes is the yarn and the machine time behind each pair. The six heights we make sets out which is which, and the two long lengths have a page of their own.

Where the 100 pairs applies, and where it does not

The minimum is not one number for one order. It attaches to a style and a colourway, which is why six designs is six commitments rather than one.

Before you cut the range to two designs

Does the 100 pairs count across the whole order?

No. It counts per style and per colourway. Six designs at the minimum is an order for 600 pairs across six programmes.

Can I split the 100 pairs across sizes?

Yes, any way you need. 25/25/25/25 across four sizes is a normal first order, and so is a single size for a team.

Are re-orders subject to the minimum too?

Yes, 100 pairs per style per colourway — but a re-order skips programming, so it is faster and cheaper than the first run.
