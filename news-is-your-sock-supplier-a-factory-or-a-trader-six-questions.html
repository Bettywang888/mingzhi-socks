Is your sock supplier a factory or a trader? Six questions that decide it

One of the most-repeated questions on r/Alibaba is how to tell whether a supplier is a factory or a trading company. The usual answer is that most "factories" on a sourcing platform are traders, and that the verified badge does not tell you which is which. That is broadly right, and it is not a scandal — a good trader can be the better choice. The problem is only when you think you are buying from a knitter and you are not.

You do not need a factory audit to tell the difference. Ask questions that only someone standing next to a machine can answer.

What gauge is the machine for this design, and what changes if we go one step finer? Any knitter knows their needle count and knows what it does to the hand of the fabric. A trader answers with adjectives.

Who closes the toe, and by what method? Linking, hand-linking and seaming give visibly different toes and different costs. It is the first thing a knitter mentions and the last thing a trader understands.

Which machine will my order run on? Not "our machines" — which one, how many needles, how many feeders. A vague answer means the order is going somewhere else.

Who dyes the yarn, and how long does a Pantone match take? A factory knows its dyehouse by name and knows the queue. A trader knows neither.

Can I see the floor on a video call, walking, on the day you say production starts? The single most useful test there is. A sales office will offer a curated tour later.

What is your full-inspection rate, and who signs the inspection sheet? This is not an accusation, it is a normal question. The answer tells you whether quality control exists as a process or as a promise.

None of these need access. All of them need the person answering to have actually been on the floor.

One honest caveat. A trading company is sometimes the right partner: if you are consolidating socks with three other categories, or you want one invoice and one forwarder, a good trader earns their margin. What you should not do is pay factory-direct prices for trader-level control, or believe you have audited a factory when you have audited a sales office.
