The line

Grip is a process, not a print.

A grip sock fails in two ways: the dots peel off, or they are placed where the foot does not load. Both are process problems, and both are visible in a sample before you order.

We knit the base sock, then apply the grip in-house — which means we can change dot size, spacing, hardness and layout without a third party. If your logo needs to sit inside the grip area, silicone handles fine detail best; PVC is the cheaper route at high volume.

Ask for: a grip comparison sample set (three processes, same base sock) with your logo in each. Most buyers decide from that, not from a spec sheet.

Grip technology

Three ways to make a sock grip — pick by volume and feel.

The grip process decides cost, durability and how the logo reads. We apply all three in-house.

Silicone

Heat-pressed into the knit. Sharpest logo detail, best wet-grip, highest cost. Standard for football and Pilates retail.

PVC dots

Screen-applied, cheapest at volume, can be built up in 3D. Slight shine and firmer hand-feel. Common for promotional and high-volume retail programmes.

Flocking

Softest touch, matte finish, good on terry-loop soles. Lower abrasion resistance — best for studio and home use.

Layouts we run

Full sole · forefoot only · heel-lock · arch band · your own pattern. Send a sketch or a photo of a competitor sock and we will match or improve it.

Durability target

We test grip retention through repeated wash cycles and can share the method and results with your QC team before you place the order.
