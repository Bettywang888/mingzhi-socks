Before you write

We are the wrong factory for three kinds of long sock.

Compression-class and medical hosiery. Those need a stated mmHg, a test report and, in most markets, a medical device registration. We hold none of that, and we will not print a compression class on a sock. If the product is a medical garment, say so and we will point you elsewhere rather than waste your sampling budget.

Colourways under 100 pairs. The floor is the machine time, not the paperwork, and it does not move for a first order — on a thigh-high either, where the yarn take is at its highest.

Off-the-shelf stock. Everything is made to your specification, so there is no warehouse to ship from this week.

Where the cost sits

Length changes the pair, not the minimum.

A thigh-high can use three times the yarn of an ankle sock and hold the machine far longer. That is a cost difference, and it is the first thing a buyer assumes is a minimum difference. It is not.

The yarn. A knee-high adds 25 to 35 centimetres of leg fabric over a crew sock; a thigh-high adds more than twice that again. Yarn is the largest per-pair cost after machine time, and it scales with length before the design does.

The machine time. The needle cylinder turns for longer and the sock is heavier at the take-down, which slows the run. The design has not changed at all, but the pair costs more to make than the same design on an ankle sock.

The band. This is where a long sock is won or lost, and it is a different problem at each length. A knee-high can sit on the calf; an over-the-calf sits above it; a thigh-high has nothing under it at all and the band carries everything. Depth, tension and the finished edge are set on the first pair against your measurements, not copied from a template.

Where they go

Four kinds of order, four different briefs.

The same machine runs all of them, but the questions are not the same.

Team kit. Football, rugby and riding ask for over-the-calf rather than knee-high, because the sock has to cover the shin pad and stay over it. Jacquard stripes and a knitted club mark survive industrial washing; embroidery reads better in a photograph.

School uniform. Six or seven sizes, one or two colourways, a mark small enough to pass a uniform code. The size run is the whole job here, and it is why the calf measurement matters more than the artwork.

Equestrian and outdoor. Heavier yarn, a deeper rib, and a band that has to survive being pulled on over a boot sock. Terry inside the sole is common on this brief.

Thigh-high. Dance and performance, bridal and occasion, costume and streetwear, and winter retail. The brief is usually about the band and the length rather than the pattern, and it is the length where a cheap band shows first.

Questions buyers ask

Before you send artwork

Straight answers, no sales copy.

Is the minimum different for a knee-high or a thigh-high?

No. 100 pairs per style per colour, on every height. The floor is the machine setup, and the setup does not care how long the sock is. What length changes is the cost of each pair, because yarn and machine time both climb with it.

So what does a thigh-high cost compared with a crew sock?

More per pair, for a simple reason: a thigh-high uses roughly three times the yarn of an ankle sock and holds the machine for proportionally longer, and the top band is an extra operation. The design has not changed at all. We quote a band for the quantity you name rather than a price per pair, because the same sock at 100 pairs and at 5,000 pairs is two different numbers.

Will a thigh-high actually stay up?

That is the whole design of one. A knee-high can lean on the calf; a thigh-high has nothing under it, so the top band carries the entire load — depth, tension and how the edge is finished are decided on the first pair, against your measurements rather than a template. It is the first thing we ask about on a thigh-high brief.

What measurements do you need?

For a knee-high, foot length plus calf circumference at the widest point. For a thigh-high, add the length from the floor to where the band should sit, and the thigh circumference at that height. Most size charts only carry foot length, which is fine for a crew sock and useless for a long one.

Do you make over-the-calf as well?

Yes — it is the same programme with a different length on the machine, and it is what most football, rugby and riding kit actually asks for. Say which length at brief stage, because the band position and the yarn take both change with it.

Can the logo be embroidered?

Yes, on the outer calf or just below the band, where there is enough flat fabric for a stitched mark of up to 8 thread colours. Embroidery on a long sock reads better than on a short one because there is room for a proper badge.

What about a school programme with six sizes?

The size run sits inside the 100 pairs, so six sizes across 100 pairs is normal. What multiplies the order is the second colourway, not the second size — each colourway is its own programme.
